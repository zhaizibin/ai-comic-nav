# Anomaly-First Analysis

Use when something about the case does not fit expectations. The anomaly is the entry point, not the conclusion.

## Goal

Find the highest-leverage tension: the detail that, if explained, would explain the most about the case.

## Anomaly Types

- Timing anomaly: too early, too late, or suspiciously well-timed.
- Actor anomaly: an expected actor is absent, silent, or unexpectedly present.
- Cost anomaly: a party accepts costs that do not fit the surface explanation.
- Sequence anomaly: the order of events implies preparation, knowledge, or dependency.
- Information anomaly: a party appears to know too much or too little.
- Scale anomaly: the response is too large or too small for the stated trigger.
- Narrative anomaly: a story leaves out the most relevant point or overexplains a weak point.

## Procedure

1. List candidate anomalies without explaining them yet.
2. For each, state the expected pattern and actual deviation.
3. Rank by explanatory leverage.
4. Choose the top one or two anomalies as analysis entry points.
5. Route to causal, incentive, or game-theory methods.
